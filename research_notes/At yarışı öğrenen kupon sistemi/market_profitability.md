# Can high-takeout pari-mutuel horse betting be beaten, and what decides it? Sceptical notes for a Turkish TJK bettor

Source labels used below:
- **[P]** primary: journal, working paper, regulator, statute, operator's own document, or a self-report by the operator.
- **[J]** major press investigation.
- **[T]** trade press or an industry advocate. Often has an interest in the outcome.
- **[S]** secondary or unverified: search snippet, law-firm blog, tipster or marketing site. Never treated as evidence.

Access limits: several sources were read only as abstracts or search excerpts, and each is flagged. TJK's own site timed out (HTTP 504) on 31 Oct 2026, so its current rule pages could not be read.

## Favourite-longshot bias and efficiency in exotic pools

### Takeaway
The favourite-longshot bias (FLB) is real and large in US data, but it mostly tells you where you lose least. On its own it does not produce a profit after takeout. Exotic pools appear to inherit misperceptions from win pools, and the evidence on trifectas is unclear. I found no test of multi-race jackpot pools such as Pick 6 or Altılı.

### Cited Findings
**Size of the bias**
- US races 1992-2001: bets at 100/1 or longer returned about -61%, random betting about -23%, and always backing the favourite about -5.5%. Horses at 4/1 to 9/1 returned a roughly constant -18% — [P] [Snowberg & Wolfers, NBER 15923](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w15923/w15923.pdf).
- So in that data even the best simple strategy, backing favourites, still lost money — [P] [same paper](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w15923/w15923.pdf).
- Ali's harness-racing work on 20,247 races found bettors overweight low-probability horses and underweight high-probability ones. I read the abstract of a 2008 reprint only, because the [JPE listing](https://ideas.repec.org/a/ucp/jpolec/v85y1977i4p803-15.html) has no abstract — [P] [reprint abstract](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:wsi:wschap:9789812819192_0011).
- Thaler & Ziemba (1988) is the classic review, arguing that wagering markets suit efficiency tests because each bet has a definite end value. I read the abstract only — [P] [JEP listing](https://scilit.com/publications/49435b79d63d76343e388507fbadc288). Ziemba's 2023 survey [revisits the field](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:anr:refeco:v:15:y:2023:p:641-662).

**The bias is not universal**
- Busche & Hall found no evident FLB in 2,653 Hong Kong races. Busche (1994) confirmed this in 2,690 more Hong Kong races and 1,738 Japanese races — [P] [cited in Snowberg & Wolfers, fn. 1](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w15923/w15923.pdf).
- A Nottingham dissertation also asks about FLB in Hong Kong. It is unpublished and I did not read it — [P, low weight] [eprints](https://eprints.nottingham.ac.uk/21025).

**Exotics**
- Using 206,808 US races with exotic payoffs, Snowberg & Wolfers found that probability misperception prices exactas, quinellas and trifectas better than risk-love. For exactas the improvement was about 18.4% — [P] [NBER 15923](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w15923/w15923.pdf).
- Their method is limited for bets priced above 200/1, which constrains the trifecta analysis most — [P] [same paper](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w15923/w15923.pdf).
- A study of 11,194 races at 35 US tracks found the standard FLB in exactas and unclear results for trifectas — [P, abstract only] [Gramm, McKinney & Owens 2008](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/applec/v40y2008i1p89-97.html).
- Finnish and Swedish harness data also favour misperception — [P, abstract only] [Empirical Economics 2019](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/empeco/v57y2019i6d10.1007_s00181-018-1538-0.html).
- In a Hong Kong win/quinella test, betting volume significantly affected efficiency across races but not within races — [P, excerpt only] [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/h/wsi/wschap/9789812819192_0043.html).
- Benter gives the pro-exotic argument: with probabilities that differ from the public's, "the more exotic (i.e. specific) the bet, the higher the advantage". His exotic returns were generally higher than his simple-pool returns — [P, self-report] [Benter 1994](https://www.gwern.net/doc/statistics/decision/1994-benter.pdf).
- Hausch, Lo & Ziemba treat the win market as essentially weak-form efficient and use it to price exotics and flag positive-expectation bets — [P, excerpt only] [EconPapers index](https://econpapers.repec.org/bookchap/wsiwsbook/6910.htm).

**Timing and information**
- Ottaviani & Sørensen explain FLB through small informed bettors who wait until the end, so the final odds reveal information. I read this through abstracts and excerpts; the full-text fetch failed — [P] [CEPR DP4092](https://cepr.org/publications/dp4092).
- Asch, Malkiel & Quandt found evidence of an informed class of bettors at Atlantic City — [P, abstract] [JFE 1982](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:10:y:1982:i:2:p:187-194).
- They later reported that they could not devise a profitable win-betting strategy, although place and show strategies could work — [P, abstract] [J. Business 1986](https://ideas.repec.org/a/ucp/jnlbus/v59y1986i1p157-60.html).

**Non-evidence**
- Green, Lee & Rothschild propose that morning lines embed the bias through deception. This is a model plus US data and does not bear on Turkey — [P, excerpt] [working paper](https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~aldous/157/Papers/Green.pdf).

### Inferences
- Do not assume FLB at TJK. It must be measured, because Hong Kong and Japan are documented exceptions.
- An Altılı pool is a different market from a win pool. Money there reflects coverage choices and ticket-building habits, so win-pool FLB results do not transfer directly.
- If the public compounds misperceived leg probabilities, mispricing could grow across legs. That is the theoretical case for jackpots, and it is unverified for TJK.

### Gaps
- I found no published efficiency test of multi-race jackpots (Pick 6, Altılı, WIN5).
- I did not obtain the full texts of Ali, Thaler & Ziemba, Gramm et al., or Asch & Quandt's ["Efficiency and Profitability in Exotic Bets"](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w15923/w15923.pdf) (listed in the Snowberg & Wolfers references).

## Documented profitable operators, and what it cost them

### Takeaway
Profit has been documented, but almost entirely through self-reports and journalism about very large operations. These operations combined years of work, deep pools and a two-stage model that used the public odds. Nothing in the record suggests a part-time bettor in a small pool can repeat this.

### Cited Findings
**Benter's own 1994 report (self-reported, no audit)**
- He reports five years of operation in Hong Kong, with four profitable seasons and one losing season that cost about 20% of starting capital. He says no precise significance calculation was made — [P, self-report] [Benter 1994](https://www.gwern.net/doc/statistics/decision/1994-benter.pdf).
- Inputs: about 2,000 races with full past-performance records at the start, about 470 races bet per year, average take about 19%, and pools above US$10 million per race — [same](https://www.gwern.net/doc/statistics/decision/1994-benter.pdf).
- Effort: about five man-years to reach a significant edge and five more to reach high profitability, with several full-time staff to keep it running — [same](https://www.gwern.net/doc/statistics/decision/1994-benter.pdf).
- Capacity: his simulations put maximum expected profit at about 0.25-0.5% of per-race turnover, even with effectively infinite capital, and unlikely above 1.5% at small tracks. A start-up might win 0.1-0.2% of turnover, and small-volume tracks probably could not support an operation — [same](https://www.gwern.net/doc/statistics/decision/1994-benter.pdf).
- Small pools also have volatile odds, which hurts any model that must bet minutes before post — [same](https://www.gwern.net/doc/statistics/decision/1994-benter.pdf).
- Other computer handicappers are especially bad for you because correlated models lower the same dividends. He also warns that past edge does not guarantee future edge — [same](https://www.gwern.net/doc/statistics/decision/1994-benter.pdf).

**Bloomberg investigation (2018)**
- Benter and Woods lost $120,000 of a $150,000 stake in their first Hong Kong season (1985-86) — [J] [Chellel, Bloomberg, mirror](https://sites.oxy.edu/lengyel/m150/Horse%20race%20betting%20%282018%29/The%20Gambler%20Who%20Cracked%20the%20Horse-Racing%20Code%20-%20Bloomberg.htm). The [original](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2018-05-03/the-gambler-who-cracked-the-horse-racing-code) was not read directly.
- Using public odds as an input was the breakthrough, and later models used over 120 variables per horse — [J] [same](https://sites.oxy.edu/lengyel/m150/Horse%20race%20betting%20%282018%29/The%20Gambler%20Who%20Cracked%20the%20Horse-Racing%20Code%20-%20Bloomberg.htm).
- Reported results: about $600,000 in his first season back, about $3 million in 1990-91, Woods $10 million in 1994-95, and the team up more than $50 million in 1997. Benter conceded close to $1 billion overall, some of it going to partners. Woods's estate was about A$939 million — [J] [same](https://sites.oxy.edu/lengyel/m150/Horse%20race%20betting%20%282018%29/The%20Gambler%20Who%20Cracked%20the%20Horse-Racing%20Code%20-%20Bloomberg.htm).
- Jackpot case: on 6 Nov 2001 the Triple Trio carried at least HK$100 million after six rollovers. Benter bet HK$1.6 million on 51,381 combinations and the win was about $16 million, which he did not collect — [J] [same](https://sites.oxy.edu/lengyel/m150/Horse%20race%20betting%20%282018%29/The%20Gambler%20Who%20Cracked%20the%20Horse-Racing%20Code%20-%20Bloomberg.htm).
- Failures and frictions: a baseball model only broke even after three summers, weather data added nothing, and the Club suspended Benter's and Woods's phone accounts in June 1997. Hong Kong tax authorities also pursued Woods's syndicate, and competition eroded Hong Kong profits by the late 1990s — [J] [same](https://sites.oxy.edu/lengyel/m150/Horse%20race%20betting%20%282018%29/The%20Gambler%20Who%20Cracked%20the%20Horse-Racing%20Code%20-%20Bloomberg.htm).
- The article does not mention rebates to Benter — [J] [same](https://sites.oxy.edu/lengyel/m150/Horse%20race%20betting%20%282018%29/The%20Gambler%20Who%20Cracked%20the%20Horse-Racing%20Code%20-%20Bloomberg.htm). The HKJC's own rebate programme began only in [September 2006](https://www.IFHAonline.org/resources/WorldMedia/ICHA_2007/Chang_2007E.pdf).

**Earlier academic systems**
- Bolton & Chapman (1986) proposed the multinomial logit model. Accounts differ on its result: [a summary of the abstract](https://medium.com/parimutuel-racetrack-analysis/ruth-n-bolton-randall-g-chapman-1986-searching-for-positive-returns-at-the-track-a-bbc2f7788e68) says it appeared to promise positive returns, while [a commentary](https://cdcgaming.com/commentary/syndicates-algorithms-and-beating-the-horses-in-hong-kong/) says the edge did not beat the house margin. Both are [S]. I did not read the original.
- Hausch, Ziemba & Rubinstein showed weak inefficiency in place and show pools — [P, excerpt] [Management Science 1981](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:inm:ormnsc:v:27:y:1981:i:12:p:1435-1452). A later paper asks how much can be bet before the inefficiency closes — [P, excerpt] [Management Science 1985](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:inm:ormnsc:v:31:y:1985:i:4:p:381-394).
- Benter doubts such technical systems can support a full-time professional effort at any track, because the chances are infrequent and the profit modest — [P] [Benter 1994](https://www.gwern.net/doc/statistics/decision/1994-benter.pdf).

**Non-evidence**
- Commentary estimates that a model and computing setup can cost over $1 million and that about 20% of US race bets come from syndicates. Neither figure is sourced — [S] [CDC Gaming](https://cdcgaming.com/commentary/syndicates-algorithms-and-beating-the-horses-in-hong-kong/).
- Net-worth figures of $1-1.5 billion from [CasinoBeats](https://casinobeats.com/features/bill-benter-net-worth/) are not evidence.

### Inferences
- **Survivorship:** we read about Benter and Woods because they survived. I found no denominator of failed model operators, so the success rate is unknown.
- **Capital and luck:** their own first season lost 80% of the stake, and Benter survived because he could recapitalise.
- **Capacity:** on Benter's numbers, even a very good start-up targets only tiny shares of turnover. A bettor on a fixed budget of a few thousand lira per card faces a different problem, where variance matters far more than capacity.
- **Carryover matters:** the best-documented jackpot case was a large rollover, attacked with broad coverage.
- **Test design:** the two-stage public-plus-model structure, holdout tests and fractional staking are the transferable lessons, not the headline dollar figures.

### Gaps
- I found no audited performance record for any operator.
- I found no systematic data on how many syndicates failed.
- Current syndicate rebate terms in Hong Kong beyond the trade-press figures below were not found.

## Computer-assisted wagering (CAW), late odds and rebates

### Takeaway
In North America, rebated computer teams pay a far lower effective takeout and move prices late. Regulators' documents and trade analyses suggest ordinary bettors lose some return, but the size is estimated rather than proven. I found no evidence that CAW operates in TJK pools, though the structural question for Turkey is what late AGF and odds moves really tell you.

### Cited Findings
**Who pays what**
- At NYRA, Superfecta takeout is 24% and teams often receive about a 17% rebate. CAW host fees at premium US tracks typically run 6-8%, and the rule of thumb is that rebate equals takeout minus host fee — [T] [TDN](https://wp.tdn.pmadv.com/computer-assisted-wagering-different-worlds-different-fixes/).
- In Hong Kong the Triple Trio takeout is 25% with a 19% host fee, so rebates are likely near zero and at most 2-3% (per Michael Fitzsimons, quoted by TDN) — [T] [TDN](https://wp.tdn.pmadv.com/computer-assisted-wagering-different-worlds-different-fixes/).

**How much of the pool**
- CAW share is about 13% of Hong Kong handle overall and about 15% of Quinella. In Ohio it is "close to" 40%. TOC's Bill Nader calls about 25% the tipping point in California, and some pools exceed it. These are not comparable measures — [T] [TDN](https://wp.tdn.pmadv.com/computer-assisted-wagering-different-worlds-different-fixes/).
- CAW groups exceeded 32% of handle in trifecta, superfecta and pick-3/4/5/6 pools in California, and 38% of Del Mar's pick five. The source is an advocacy report from the Thoroughbred Idea Foundation — [T] [TDN on TIF](https://wp.tdn.pmadv.com/tif-issues-alarming-report-on-computer-assisted-wagering-in-ca/).

**Impact on ordinary bettors**
- A September 2024 document for the California Horse Racing Board estimated that Elite Turf Club's CAW play adds 2.5% to effective takeout for retail players. It also gives ownership as Stronach Group 80%, NYRA 20%. I saw this through BloodHorse's report, not the document itself — [T citing regulator document] [TrueNicks/BloodHorse](https://www.truenicks.com/articles/286121/del-mar-to-close-win-pool-to-caw-at-two-minutes-to-post).
- PMU reported that after barring international players from Quinté+, a Prix d'Amérique winning ticket was projected to rise from about €640,000 to about €1.2 million. This is an operator projection — [T] [TDN](https://wp.tdn.pmadv.com/computer-assisted-wagering-different-worlds-different-fixes/).

**Late moves**
- At Del Mar 2025, 15% of the win pool was bet in the final cycle (16.1% the year before). Gramm counted 40 winners shortening late and 30 drifting, and one winner went from 18-1 to 6-1 — [T, from a social-media post by an economist who also bets] [BloodHorse](https://www.truenicks.com/articles/286121/del-mar-to-close-win-pool-to-caw-at-two-minutes-to-post).
- Gramm & McKinney reported CAW last-cycle share rising since 2022 at Aqueduct, with place and show up 23.2% and 22.7%. The study first missed a change in NYRA's tote that extended the last cycle from 10 to 30 seconds, and the corrected numbers were lower — [T] [TDN, 18 July 2025](https://www.thoroughbreddailynews.com/pdf/tdn/2025/tdn250718.pdf).
- Hanyu, Ishii, Otani & Teramoto, using interim odds from Japanese racing, found returns negatively related to last-minute odds changes, and that late moves weaken the odds-return link and so attenuate FLB. It is a preprint, and I read the abstract only — [P] [arXiv 2509.14645](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.14645v1).

**Regulatory response**
- NYRA cut CAW win betting at three minutes to post, then added guardrails in February 2026. Its internal study shows average late-odds volatility falling from 5.95 (January) to 2.59 (11 Feb-25 May), and CAW share from about 20-22% to about 12-13%. NYRA supplied the data and the baseline is short — [T reporting NYRA data] [BloodHorse/TrueNicks](https://truenicks.com/articles/292119/nyra-seeing-reduced-volatility-in-late-odds-changes).
- Del Mar closed win pools to CAW at two minutes from 31 July 2025 — [T] [BloodHorse](https://www.truenicks.com/articles/286121/del-mar-to-close-win-pool-to-caw-at-two-minutes-to-post).
- HKJC published color-coded boards to reveal syndicate betting — [J] [Bloomberg mirror](https://sites.oxy.edu/lengyel/m150/Horse%20race%20betting%20%282018%29/The%20Gambler%20Who%20Cracked%20the%20Horse-Racing%20Code%20-%20Bloomberg.htm).

**Evidence against simple "CAW only hurts" claims**
- HKJC's 2006 rebate (10% of losing stake on tickets of HK$10,000 or more in four pools) coincided with 7% revenue growth, the first rise in ten years, and the Club framed it as a tool against illegal betting. That shows liquidity benefit to the operator, not to ordinary bettors — [P, operator] [Chang, HKJC, 2007](https://www.IFHAonline.org/resources/WorldMedia/ICHA_2007/Chang_2007E.pdf).
- The Cox Plate World Pool paid $2.80 against an estimated $1.70-1.80 from a local-only pool, which suggests commingled money can improve some prices. That is one race and an estimate by an interested party — [T] [TDN](https://wp.tdn.pmadv.com/computer-assisted-wagering-different-worlds-different-fixes/).

**Turkey**
- TJK's regulation defines the Altılı Ganyan Favorisi as the most-backed horse in each leg, set separately per game and announced after the first leg runs. It also serves as the substitute selection when a horse does not run — [P] [Yönetmelik, Lexpera consolidated text](https://www.lexpera.com.tr/mevzuat/yonetmelikler/at-yarislari-musterek-bahisler-yonetmeligi).
- AmTote announced agreements with TJK — [T, headline only, not read] [TrueNicks](https://truenicks.com/articles/278949/amtote-announces-agreements-with-turkish-jockey-club).

**Non-evidence**
- The claim that rebates often return half the takeout comes from an [opinion column](https://pastthewire.com/better-late-than-never-nyra-finally-draws-a-line-on-caw). It is directionally consistent with TDN's figures but is not itself evidence.

### Inferences
- NYRA both restricts CAW and, per the ownership figure above, holds a stake in a CAW platform, so its own impact data should be read with that interest in mind.
- Rebated players pay a different game's takeout. If a similar scheme exists or arrives in Turkey, ordinary bettors should treat it as raising their effective cost.
- A coupon built before the last odds cycle faces a price that can still move. Back-tests using final AGF or final odds use information that a bettor could not have had when placing the bet, unless the bet really was placed at that moment. This is a look-ahead risk for any TJK model.
- Hanyu et al. imply that late signals have value, but a bettor must be able to act on them in time.

### Gaps
- **I found no evidence on whether CAW, large automated teams or rebates exist in TJK or United Racing pools.**
- I found no public Turkish data on the timing of AGF or odds updates, or on the share of money arriving late.
- I found no peer-reviewed estimate of how much CAW reduces ordinary bettors' returns. The 2.5% figure is an estimate I could not read in the original.
- TIF report, NYRA and TDN pages returned HTTP 403 at their main URLs, so I relied on mirrors and the TDN PDF.
- Nothing usable was found on Korean CAW or takeout.

## Takeout by product in Turkey, Turkish research and comparable markets

### Takeaway
I could not find a current official takeout table by product. The best available evidence, from a 2021 trade magazine that attributes its figures to TJK, implies that about 50% goes to players on the standard breakdown, while ganyan, ikili and çifte paid 65-75% during 2020. **I found no Turkish academic study of TJK or AGF market efficiency.** Betting rights also moved to a new operator on 1 January 2026, so older figures may no longer apply.

### Cited Findings
**Turkish rates**
- TJK said in June 2020 that it would keep distributing 75% of sales on ganyan, ikili and çifte — [T quoting TJK] [A Spor](https://www.aspor.com.tr/diger-sporlar/2020/06/16/tjk-bahislerin-yuzde-75ini-dagitacak).
- Breakdown per 100 TL of sales: KDV 15.25, belediye eğlence vergisi 4.24, şans oyunları vergisi 5.93, kamu payı 2.54, müessese hissesi 22.03, leaving 50 TL for players — [T] [Yarış Dergisi, 28 Mar 2021](https://www.yarisdergisi.com/altili-ganyan-iniste-ganyan-plase-ikili-ve-cifte-cikista/).
- The same article says ganyan, plase, ikili, çifte and plase ikili distributed 75%, 70% or 65% at different points in the year. It says TJK takes an equal müessese hissesi on all games, and gives Altılı's turnover share as 54.80% (2017), 54.58% (2018), 49.15% (2019) and 42.45% (2020) — [T] [same](https://www.yarisdergisi.com/altili-ganyan-iniste-ganyan-plase-ikili-ve-cifte-cikista/).
- **Conflict in that source:** the listed deductions total 50 TL, so a 75% payout cannot carry all of them. The article does not name the game the 50 TL applies to. Applying it to Altılı is my reading, not its statement.
- Tax on horse betting: Law 5602 Art. 6 set 7% in 2007, with the government able to double or halve it — [P] [Resmî Gazete](https://www.resmigazete.gov.tr/eskiler/2007/03/20070321-1.htm).
- It went from 14% to 7% with effect from 1 January 2024 — [T] [Dünya](https://www.dunya.com/ekonomi/bahis-ve-sans-oyunu-vergileri-dusuruldu-haberi-714738). So the rate was changed more than once after 2007.
- Prizes in the 2007 text had to be 40-59% of hasılat (revenue net of KDV), as an annual average across games, so rates can differ by game — [P] [Resmî Gazete](https://www.resmigazete.gov.tr/eskiler/2007/03/20070321-1.htm). A search excerpt of later text gives 40-83%; I did not read the current consolidated article myself — [S] [Mevzuat](https://mevzuat.gov.tr/anasayfa/MevzuatFihristDetayIframe?MevzuatNo=5602&MevzuatTertip=5&MevzuatTur=1).
- The betting regulation sets no percentage itself. It defers to the law for the amount to be distributed — [P] [Yönetmelik Art. 3(1)(f)](https://www.lexpera.com.tr/mevzuat/yonetmelikler/at-yarislari-musterek-bahisler-yonetmeligi).
- If nobody holds the winning group in games including Altılı, the amount to be distributed carries over to a following game at the same hippodrome. A partial-stake winners' share can also carry over — [P, excerpt] [Yönetmelik, TJK copy](https://medya-cdn.tjk.org/medyaftp/pdf/Musterek_Bahisler_Yonetmeligi.pdf); [Art. 18(2)](https://www.lexpera.com.tr/mevzuat/yonetmelikler/at-yarislari-musterek-bahisler-yonetmeligi).

**Operator change**
- A Turkish review article says that after a Türkiye Varlık Fonu decision, United Racing Şans Oyunları A.Ş. became sole licensee under a 10-year contract from 1 January 2026, six weeks after being founded, and that TJK announced the handover — [P, review article] [Aydın, Bursa Uludağ J. 2025](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/download/article-file/5557529). See also TJK's [farewell message](https://medya-cdn.tjk.org/medyaftp/veda_mesaji.pdf) (not read).
- Press reports differ on ownership — [T] [Cumhuriyet](https://www.cumhuriyet.com.tr/yazarlar/murat-agirel/at-yarisi-bahsinde-demiroren-ortakligi-2525504), [Haber Hürriyeti](https://www.haberhurriyeti.com/haber/28447360/bahis-sektorunde-yeni-ortaklik-zinciri-demiroren-ismi-yine-sahnede).
- A trade report that dealer commissions were cut (agents 4.5% to 2.5%, retail 6% to 5%) came through a search excerpt only — [S] [SCCG](https://sccgmanagement.com/sccg-articles/2026/08/10/turkey-horseracing-betting-transfer-draws-scrutiny-on-commissions-and-ownership-ties/). I could not confirm it.

**Turkish research**
- Searches in Turkish and English found no thesis or paper testing FLB or efficiency in TJK or AGF data. YÖK Tez and TR Dizin were not directly searchable with my tools, so this means "not found", not "does not exist".
- Closest work: a [2019 Okan University thesis on football betting efficiency](https://gcris.okan.edu.tr/items/dbec0432-e0a5-4114-9570-10c83f9364c0), and [Aydın's review](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/download/article-file/5557529) of the sector's history and economics, which is not an efficiency test. A thesis, Helvacı (2024), on the socio-economics of racing is cited in Aydın's bibliography, but I did not read it.

### Inferences
Arithmetic of mine, not sourced findings:
- **Jackpot break-even from carryover:** if players receive fraction r of new handle H and carryover is C, the pool's average return is (rH + C)/H. At r = 0.50, average break-even needs C of at least 0.50H. Below that, the average ticket loses, before considering skill or shared winners. So the ratio of devir to expected handle is a key number to log.
- **Skill break-even:** with r = 0.50 and no carryover, a coupon must be about 2^(1/6) = 1.122 times as likely as the public's staking implies on each of six legs. A win bet at r = 0.75 needs 1.333 on one race. This supports Benter's point about specific bets. It does not make the Altılı easier in practice: the edge must hold on every leg of the combinations actually bought, ticket cost and split jackpots reduce it further, and legs may not be independent.
- **Measure, don't assume:** the realised rate for each game can be estimated from the operator's published pools and dividends. Comparing periods before and after 1 January 2026 would show whether the rate changed.
- **Current breakdown:** KDV and tax rates have changed since 2021, so the 50 TL figure should not be treated as current.

## Responsible limits: losing streaks, problem gambling and Turkish rules

### Takeaway
Even the best-documented professionals had severe drawdowns and warn against overestimating edge. Account data show losses are highly concentrated, and most bettors reduce activity after losses, while chasing marks the minority most at risk. Turkish prevalence data on horse betting is thin.

### Cited Findings
**Staking and drawdowns**
- Benter warns that overestimating the edge by more than a factor of two makes Kelly betting produce negative capital growth, that this is easily done in practice, and that real-time edge is rarely better than simulated. Full Kelly commonly produces downswings above 50% of wealth, so he recommends fractional Kelly such as 1/2 or 1/3 — [P] [Benter 1994](https://www.gwern.net/doc/statistics/decision/1994-benter.pdf).
- A losing season of about 20% of capital occurred with fractional staking, and with the skill and pool depth described earlier — [P] [same](https://www.gwern.net/doc/statistics/decision/1994-benter.pdf).

**Behaviour after losses**
- Finnish horse-bettor data (over 5,000 bettors) found reduced risk-taking after losses, with a goal of at least breaking even. This runs against the common chasing assumption — [T, press summary of a Management Science paper] [Phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2017-05-losses-subsequent-bettorsapplies-risky-decisions.pdf).
- After a losing day, online horse bettors waited about 27% longer before their next betting day than after a break-even day — [P] [Kainulainen 2021, J. Gambling Studies](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32948929/).
- Chasing measures were strongly related to excessive gambling and impaired control among off-course race bettors and gaming-machine players — [P, abstract] [O'Connor & Dickerson 2003](https://researchers.westernsydney.edu.au/en/publications/definition-and-measurement-of-chasing-in-off-course-betting-and-g/).
- Players sped up after losses in real gambling, and the authors suggest this may reflect an urge to continue — [P, abstract] [PLOS ONE](https://ideas.repec.org/a/plo/pone00/0273359.html).

**Concentration and limits**
- In Norsk Rikstoto account data for 2021-2023, the top 20% of bettors accounted for 86% of losses overall. I read this through a page reproducing the abstract, because the journal page was unavailable — [P via secondary] [Buzcular et al. 2026](https://madbarn.com/research/distribution-of-losses-among-horse-race-bettors-an-analysis-of-account-based-player-tracking-data/).
- Norway has been considering or applying a 20,000 NOK monthly loss limit for horse bettors. Details were not read — [T] [Harness Racing Update](https://harnessracingupdate.com/?p=167588); related study [J. Gambling Studies 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10899-025-10446-3) (redirected to login, not read).

**Turkey**
- A national survey of 5,008 people aged 15+ in 12 provinces found 6.81% had gambled at least once. The abstract gives no problem-gambling rate and does not mention horse racing — [P] [Altıntaş et al. 2024](https://openaccess.ihu.edu.tr/entities/publication/d743214d-cecd-42da-984a-c3b84b833f12/full).
- Northern Cyprus surveys report 2.2% lifetime probable pathological gambling (2012), and 3.5% plus 9.2% probable problem gambling (2016). This is not Turkey proper — [P, excerpts] [Çakıcı, Alpha Psychiatry](https://alpha-psychiatry.com/en/the-prevalence-and-risk-factors-of-gambling-behavior-in-turkish-republic-of-northern-cyprus-131222); [2016 study](https://www.springermedicine.com/lifetime-of-prevalence-and-risk-factors-of-problem-and-pathologi/21662292).
- A METU thesis sampled 357 men at sports and horse-race betting terminals. It is a convenience sample, read as an excerpt only — [P] [METU](https://open.metu.edu.tr/handle/11511/21532).
- Turkish rule: betting sales must be paid in advance, and credit-card, instalment or deferred payment is not allowed — [P] [Yönetmelik Art. 4(1)(b)](https://www.lexpera.com.tr/mevzuat/yonetmelikler/at-yarislari-musterek-bahisler-yonetmeligi).
- Betting outside the licensed framework falls outside Law 6132 — [S, search excerpt] [Law 6132](https://www.mevzuat.gov.tr/MevzuatMetin/1.3.6132.pdf). I did not confirm the specific article.

### Inferences
- Treat a positive back-test as weak evidence. Benter's warning implies staking should assume the true edge may be half or less of the estimate, or zero.
- With a jackpot's long-tailed payoff, long losing runs are the normal case and tell you little about skill either way. The budget should be money the bettor can lose entirely, fixed in advance, and not raised after losses.
- Practical warning signs from the literature: raising stakes after losses, betting faster, and betting beyond a preset limit. The average bettor backing off after a loss does not protect the minority who do not.
- Absence of Turkish evidence is not reassurance.

### Gaps
- No nationally representative Turkish figure for horse-betting problem gambling was found.
- I did not find a losing-streak study specific to jackpot pools.
- Turkish age limits, self-exclusion tools and official support services were not confirmed from an official source in this research, so none are stated here.
- No official Turkish responsible-gambling rules for United Racing were found.
